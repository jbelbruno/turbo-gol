## 7. Modes and connections

1. **CASUAL** needs an account and is always played on a server, with the game's official
   physics. We look for players in your region first and, if nobody shows up within 1 minute, in
   every region. As soon as 2 or more people are searching, the match starts and bots fill the free
   seats; the format depends on how many they are. If nobody comes within 5 minutes, you play bots
   on a server, in the format you picked, if the servers have room and the game is in view
   (otherwise, as soon as there is room or you are back in the game). While you wait you can play
   bots or train in your browser: when your match is found you join it at once and that local match
   is lost. CASUAL never counts for the rating.
2. **Lobbies are played P2P:** your device connects directly to the devices of the other players
   in the lobby, including people you do not know. To set up that connection, each device receives
   the public IP address of the others. From an IP it is possible to tell, roughly, which city you
   are in and who your internet provider is. It is the same whether you create the lobby or join
   someone else's. **If you do not want other players to see your IP, do not play in lobbies, or
   play only with people you know.** CASUAL and COMPETITIVE are never P2P.
3. **COMPETITIVE** is always played on a server, with the game's official physics. It needs an
   account. We search your region first and, after 2 minutes, every region. The result changes
   your rating. The top 100 of each mode, with at least 5 matches in the season, are shown with
   their username on a public leaderboard. Today there is no option to hide your name on it. If you
   ask for your account to be deleted, you stop appearing on it right away.
4. **Custom lobbies.** Whoever creates a lobby is the lobby host (they can hand the role to another
   member): they choose the mode and the rules, and the title if they have an account; they can set
   a password and they can remove a player. When you create a lobby you choose whether it is
   public (CREATE PUBLIC LOBBY) or private (CREATE PRIVATE LOBBY), and the lobby host can change
   it. Lobbies are always played P2P (section 7.2) and a match needs at least 2 players. A public
   lobby appears in a list that anyone can see, with its title, the name of its current host, its
   code, the mode, the region, how many players it has, whether it has a password and, if it has
   any, its experimental rules. Lobby matches never count for the rating, are not played on
   community servers and are not published as replays.
5. **Experimental features and rules.** They are for accounts, they may change or disappear and
   **they count for nothing**: no rating, no stats, no records.
6. **Servers.** Server matches are played on Cloudflare servers or on community servers (section
   12). Today the only community server is the project's, in São Paulo, and only the project can
   register servers. A CASUAL or COMPETITIVE match is played in the region of most of its players
   (on a tie, the region of whoever waited longest): with a South American majority it goes to the
   project's server in São Paulo when it has room; otherwise, and for the other regions, to a
   Cloudflare server: we ask Cloudflare to place it near that region, but Cloudflare decides
   where. If in an emergency we turn off matches on Cloudflare servers, every CASUAL or
   COMPETITIVE match is played on the project's server in São Paulo, whatever its region, and
   waits until it has room (or, if that server is not available either, you cannot search for a
   match).

## 8. COMPETITIVE: abandons and verification

1. **It counts as an abandon** if you leave a COMPETITIVE match, if you do not show up or, on a
   Cloudflare server or a project server, if you are disconnected when the match ends. You cannot
   search for a COMPETITIVE match for:
   - 5 minutes, the first time in 24 hours;
   - 15 minutes, the second;
   - 1 hour, the third;
   - 24 hours, from the fourth on.

   Also, an abandon counts as a loss in your rating, even if your team wins.
2. **While you wait for COMPETITIVE playing bots or training,** the game takes you into your match
   as soon as it is found. With the tab in the background you join all the same and the match is
   played even if you are not watching: the result counts. If the tab is closed or the device asleep
   and you never connect, it counts as not showing up (section 8.1).
3. The game decides these blocks on its own. You can ask for a person to review them (section 9).
4. **COMPETITIVE matches on community servers are verified** by simulating the match again. If one
   fails verification, or cannot be verified in time, its result does not count.

3. **Your username is shown** in the list and in the replay. If you do not want it shown, turn on

4. If you want us to take a replay down, write to us at <mailto:privacy@turbo-gol.com>.
5. If you have an account, you can star a replay and see your matches in "My replays". For each
   replay we show how many accounts starred it, not which ones.

### 12.4 What is public and what we keep about you

1. **It is public,** for anyone who asks for it at turbo-gol.com/nodes/status: your username as a

2. **Your server's address may be your IP.** The players who connect see it.
3. **We keep:** each server's data (including the machine's data and how many matches it verified,
   lost or failed), the IP addresses it connects from (30 days) and your points. A server stays in
   our records even if it is shut down. The Privacy Policy says for how long.

### 12.5 Points

1. One point for each minute a real player played on your server, in verified matches, with factors
   for region, quality and repetition. The exact rules are in Annex A, at the end of these terms.
2. A new server receives only casual matches, and all of them are verified, until it reaches 20
   verified matches. After that, a random share of the casual matches is picked to be verified
   (today, one in five): only those give points, and they are worth more to make up for it.
   COMPETITIVE matches are all verified.
3. Your own minutes do not count. Guests and new accounts count for less.
4. Beyond that, the rules of section 11 apply.

## Annex A. How host points are calculated

These are today's values. Section 11.7 says how they can change.

1. **Base:** one point for each minute a real player was in a match on your server. The minutes are
   counted by the verifier when it simulates the match again, not by your server. Stretches of 10
   seconds or more in which the player touches no control do not count; that is what happens, for
   example, when they disconnect.
2. **Those minutes are multiplied by:**
   - the **player weight:** 1 if they have an account that is 7 days old or more; 0.25 if they are
     a guest or a new account;
   - the **region factor:** from 0.5 to 3. It goes up where servers are missing and down where
     there are too many;
   - the **quality:** 1 if the server's latency is 12 ms or less (99th percentile) and the players'
     controls arrive on time in at least 95% of the game's steps; if not, 0.5;
   - the **repetition:** 1 divided by (1 + the number of times the same group of players played
     on your servers in the last 24 hours).
3. **Your own minutes do not count,** and the system does not assign you COMPETITIVE matches that
   you play in.
4. **Which matches are verified:** all COMPETITIVE ones. All casual ones on a new server, until it
   reaches 20 verified matches. After that, one in five casual ones, picked at random, which counts
   as five. Only verified matches give points.
5. **If a match fails verification,** it is worth zero points, your server is suspended until a
   person reviews it and your redemptions are paused.
6. The project's servers earn no points.
