## 7. Modes and connections

1. **CASUAL** needs an account and is always played on a server, with the game's official
   physics. We look for players in your region first and, if nobody shows up within 1 minute, in
   every region. Someone who has already searched for 1 minute in another region also finds you as
   soon as you start, and the match may then be played in their region (section 7.6). As soon as 2
   or more people find each other, the match starts and bots fill the free seats; the format
   depends on how many they are. If nobody comes within 5 minutes, you play bots on a server, in
   the format and at the difficulty you picked, once the game is in view (otherwise, as soon as
   you are back in the game). If the servers are full, the match, with people or with bots, waits
   until there is room. While you wait you can play bots or train in your browser: when your match
   is found you join it at once and that local match is lost. CASUAL never counts for the rating.
2. **Lobbies are played P2P:** your device connects directly to the devices of the other players
   in the lobby, including people you do not know. To set up that connection, each device receives
   the public IP address of the others. From an IP it is possible to tell, roughly, which city you
   are in and who your internet provider is. It is the same whether you create the lobby or join
   someone else's. **If you do not want other players to see your IP, do not play in lobbies, or
   play only with people you know.** CASUAL and COMPETITIVE are never P2P. But from the
   opening (section 12.1.2), a CASUAL match may be played on the server of a host who is not the
   project, who receives your IP and may play in it (section 7.6).
3. **COMPETITIVE** is always played on a server, with the game's official physics. It needs an
   account. We search your region first and, after 2 minutes, every region. The result changes
   your rating. The top 100 of each mode, with at least 5 matches in the season, are shown with
   their username on a public leaderboard. Today there is no option to hide your name on it. If you
   ask for your account to be deleted, you stop appearing on it right away.
4. **Custom lobbies.** Whoever creates a lobby is the lobby host (they can hand the role to another
   member): they choose the mode, the rules and the bots' difficulty, and the title if they have an
   account; they can set a password and they can remove a player. When you create a lobby you
   choose whether it is public (CREATE PUBLIC LOBBY) or private (CREATE PRIVATE LOBBY), and the
   lobby host can change
   it. Lobbies are always played P2P (section 7.2) and a match needs at least 2 players. A public
   lobby appears in a list that anyone can see, with its title, the name of its current host, its
   code, the mode, the region, how many players it has, whether it has a password and, if it has
   any, its experimental rules. Lobby matches never count for the rating, are not played on
   community servers and are not published as replays.
5. **Experimental features and rules.** They are for accounts, they may change or disappear and
   **they count for nothing**: no rating, no stats, no records.
6. **Servers.** Server matches are played on Cloudflare servers or on community servers (section
   12). Until the opening of the network (section 12.1.2), the only community servers are the
   project's own, in São Paulo (Brazil), Germany and Malaysia and, for now, also in South Africa,
   Australia and the United States, and only the project can register servers. From that day,
   hosts that are not the project register them too. The ones in service are listed, with their
   region, at turbo-gol.com/nodes/status (section 12.4). A CASUAL or COMPETITIVE match is played in
   the region of most of its players (on a tie, the region of whoever waited longest):
   - **CASUAL:** if that region has a community server that can take it, the match goes there. If
     there are several, it goes to one of the least loaded, picked at random, whether it is the
     project's or not;
   - **COMPETITIVE:** for now, only a project server of that region can take it, never a community
     server that is not the project's (section 8.4);
   - if none can take it (for example, because it is not connected, has no room or is not
     compatible with the game's current version), it goes to a Cloudflare server: we ask Cloudflare
     to place it near that region, but Cloudflare decides where.

   It never goes to a server of another region, except in an emergency: if we turn off matches on
   Cloudflare servers, a CASUAL match goes to a community server of its region and, if none can
   take it, to one in South America; a COMPETITIVE one, to the project server of its region and, if
   that one cannot take it, to the one in São Paulo. Either way it waits until one of them has room
   (or, if those servers are not available either, you cannot search for a match).

   **Project servers only.** From the opening, if you turn on "Project servers only" in Settings >
   Gameplay, the matches you search for from that browser or that TV never go to a community server
   that is not the project's: they go to a project server or to a Cloudflare one (in the emergency
   above, they wait for a project server). The option is kept only in that browser or on that TV,
   not in your account: turn it on in each one you play on, and again if you clear the site's data.
   If one player in the match turns it on, it applies to the whole match. MATCH FOUND and the FINAL
   tell you when the match is played on a community server that is not the project's.

## 8. COMPETITIVE: abandons and verification

1. **It counts as an abandon** if you leave a COMPETITIVE match, if you do not show up or, on a
   Cloudflare server or a project server, if you are disconnected when the match ends. You cannot
   search for a COMPETITIVE match for:
   - 5 minutes, the first time in 24 hours;
   - 15 minutes, the second;
   - 1 hour, the third;
   - 24 hours, from the fourth on.

   Also, an abandon counts as a loss in your rating, even if your team wins.
2. **While you wait for COMPETITIVE playing bots or training,** the game takes you into your match
   as soon as it is found. With the tab in the background you join all the same and the match is
   played even if you are not watching: the result counts. If the tab is closed or the device asleep
   and you never connect, it counts as not showing up (section 8.1).
3. The game decides these blocks on its own. You can ask for a person to review them (section 9).
4. **For now, COMPETITIVE matches are not played on community servers that are not the
   project's:** only on the project's or on Cloudflare's (section 7.6). Those played on a project
   server are verified by simulating the match again. If one fails verification, or cannot be
   verified in time, its result does not count.

5. If you have an account, you can star a replay and see your matches in "My replays". For each
   replay we show how many accounts starred it, not which ones.

## 12. Community server hosts

   register a server yet. The opening has no set date.
3. The project's servers earn no points. That is why, until the opening, nobody can earn points
   (section 11.1). From that day, the host of a server that is not the project's earns them

## Annex A. How server points are calculated

These are today's values. Section 11.7 says how they can change.

1. **Base:** one point for each minute a real player was in a match on your server. The minutes are
   counted by the verifier when it simulates the match again; what your server measures can only
   lower them. Stretches of 10
   seconds or more in which the player touches no control do not count; that is what happens, for
   example, when they disconnect.
2. **Those minutes are multiplied by:**
   - the **player weight:** 1 if they have an account that is 7 days old or more; 0.25 if it is a
     new account;
   - the **region factor:** from 0.5 to 3. It goes up where servers are missing and down where
     there are too many, and it also counts the capacity of the project's servers in that region;
   - the **quality:** 1 if the server's latency is 12 ms or less (99th percentile) and the players'
     controls arrive on time in at least 95% of the game's steps; if not, 0.5;
   - the **repetition:** each player counts 1 divided by (1 + the number of times they played on
     your servers in the last 24 hours), and the match, in addition, 1 divided by (1 + the number of
     times the same group of players played on your servers in the last 24 hours).
3. **Your own minutes do not count,** and for now your servers get no COMPETITIVE matches
   (section 8.4).
4. **Which matches are verified:** all those of your server while it is on probation, until it
   reaches 20 verified matches. After that, one in five, picked at random, which counts as five.
   Only verified matches give points.
5. **If a match fails verification,** it is worth zero points, your server is suspended until a
   person reviews it and your redemptions are paused.
6. **Worth zero points:** a match with a single real player (the bots match of someone who waited
   alone) and a voided match.
7. **Cap per day:** each host has a cap on points per day, adding up all their servers. What goes
   over the cap is worth zero points. MY SERVERS shows you the cap and your points of the day.
8. **Capacity caps:** a community server that is not the project's gets at most a number of
   matches at a time, lower while it is on probation, even if it declares more; and the servers of
   one host, added up, also have a cap on matches at a time.
9. The project's servers earn no points.
